Who we are

A student design.
A tested toboggan.

We are GNCTR Club at NAIT, a student-led team building a toboggan for the Great Northern Concrete Toboggan Race in Edmonton. We apply classroom learning to real engineering: concrete running surfaces, structural systems, steering and brakes that all have to work together on snow.

The project asks for more than a good idea. We make design decisions, source materials, build safely and manage a budget. Then we present our work and put it to the test.

Building on last year’s experience, we finished a large part of the design over the summer. Our next goal is to complete the first build early enough for a test run, so we can check steering, braking and rider safety before race week.

Our mission

Give students a hands-on way to apply classroom learning through a shared engineering project.

Our vision

Arrive with a safe, tested toboggan and a team ready to explain the decisions behind it.

Our values

Safety, teamwork, thoughtful design and accountability for the resources entrusted to us.

2027 project plan

Our path from design to competition.

Summer design work gave us a head start. We are using it to plan fabrication and aim for a test run before the race.

  1. Summer 2026

    Design head start

    Completed a substantial portion of the design work.

  2. Fall 2026

    Review, source & coordinate

    Review designs, seek sponsorships and plan material purchases.

  3. Nov–Dec 2026

    Fabricate & assemble

    Build components, assemble the toboggan and check fit and function.

  4. December 2026

    Test run

    Aim for a test run to check steering, braking and rider safety.

  5. January 2027

    Final adjustments & driver training

    Fine-tune the toboggan and prepare the riders for competition.

  6. Jan 27–30, 2027

    Compete in Edmonton

    Present our design, complete event checks and take part in GNCTR.

Dates are planning targets. Testing depends on build progress, shop access and safe site conditions. We aim to finish any concrete changes by late December; January focuses on final adjustments and driver training.
